01

SYneRgism

Damascus · 2022–23
ACESD Studio, MaCAD

Can a void connect a city?

A mediating tool at the urban scale.

SYneRgism brings building system, climate and urban fabric into a single computational process, and tests it in the void of the former Hejaz station in Damascus.

Context

Damascus grew westward from its agricultural land in the east. The Hejaz station split the city in two, new and old; what remains today is the ticket building and an excavated plot.

The project occupies that void with a market that brings producers and consumers together: a green square with shared spaces between the two edges.

01 / 02

Type and climate

In the arid climate of Damascus, the courtyard works as a temperature regulator. The study classifies the units the city is built with and compares courtyard geometries, square, rectangular, triangular and irregular, by their thermal behaviour.

The trench is read the same way: the void is one more module in the composition.

01 / 03

Forming the massing

The idea that organises the project is the formation of the massing through climatic computational rules. The plot is subdivided and, on that grid, an algorithm distributes solid and void: it generates hundreds of options in a density matrix and filters them by programme, experience and climate.

The options that pass the filter are studied at module scale with thermal comfort analysis. The massing comes out of those rules, and the tool at the end of this note lets you play with its logic.

01 / 04

Building system

The structure is an optimised timber grid. The module is sized together with the grid, and parts and joints are tested to tie construction, programme and public space together.

01 / 04

The market

The market sinks into the trench above all to protect itself from the Syrian sun: below street level, the walls and the timber structure give shade during the hottest hours. The programme is laid out in bands: entrances, stored market, temporal market and green shared spaces. The section brings these decisions together: going down into the market, crossing it and restoring continuity between its edges.

02

EASE

Madrid · 2023
Graph Machine Learning, MaCAD

What does one trip tell us about the next?

The everyday city is drawn by coming back.

01 / 05

EASE studies how a sequence of trips can be read as a network of places, times and recurring relations. Maps and graphs make it possible to explore the structure of those habits and to propose models of next destinations.

Predictive capacity remains a hypothesis that needs independent validation. The architectural interest lies in observing the city through use, alongside its physical form, while keeping explicit the limits of what the data can tell.

Ahmed Faizal and Alberto Carro Novo.Faculty: David Andres Leon and Dai Kandil.

Latent Studio

Barcelona · 2023
Final thesis, MaCAD

What space hides between two forms?

Between two known forms lies a field of possibilities.

01 / 05

Latent Studio explores the territory between two known forms: a catalogue of architectural volumes that lets you walk the transitions from one configuration to another. The research trains a 3D generative network on voxelised models and makes its latent space visible.

What matters is the moment before choosing a solution: recognising families, discovering variations and deciding what deserves to be developed. The catalogue widens the field of testing; architectural judgement guides the choice.

Alberto Carro Novo.Thesis supervisor: Oana Taut.
